\begin{abstract}
Autonomous cloud resilience demands proactive management platforms that forecast emerging resource anomalies, diagnose underlying drivers, and trigger remediations before critical service disruption occurs. This paper presents an explainable AI-driven autonomous cloud management platform that integrates ten sequentially connected processing stages: telemetry ingestion, anomaly detection, temporal buffer management, temporal feature extraction, multi-model failure-risk prediction, deterministic model-coefficient attribution, likely-failure-category inference, autonomous decision logic, simulated recovery, and post-recovery verification. The system is implemented as a deployable research prototype and evaluated on a controlled synthetic telemetry dataset covering eight distinct scenario profiles: two normal baseline profiles (stable and variable) and six failure scenarios (CPU spike, gradual CPU failure, memory leak, disk gradual exhaustion, network degradation, and combined gradual failure). Isolation Forest anomaly detection achieves precision 0.704, recall 0.826, and F1 0.760 on snapshot telemetry. Temporal Logistic Regression achieves mean F1 0.692 ($\pm$ 0.036) and ROC-AUC 0.813 ($\pm$ 0.021) across five random seeds, substantially outperforming both a majority-class baseline (F1 0.514) and a current-value threshold baseline (F1 0.156). Among 18 evaluated failure sequences, the model issued at least one high-risk prediction before labelled failure onset with a mean lead time of 18.06 timesteps. The XAI attribution layer computes exact coefficient-based contributions for each of 45 temporal features, enabling transparent identification of which telemetry patterns drive each prediction. A post-recovery verification mechanism re-evaluates simulated recovered telemetry using the same model, returning RECOVERED, NOT\_RECOVERED, or INCONCLUSIVE verdicts. All experiments are conducted on synthetic data; real-world deployment validation is identified as future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Distributed cloud architectures emit continuous multidimensional telemetry spanning compute, memory, storage, and networking interfaces. Under operational scale, the sheer throughput of telemetry metrics rapidly overwhelms manual operations, while the window between initial performance degradation and customer-impacting outages frequently collapses below human triage latency \cite{Lin2019AIOps}. To bridge this operational gap, Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations (AIOps) architectures have emerged to automate fault identification, root-cause diagnostics, and targeted remediation \cite{Bogatinovski2022AIOps,Niu2020DLAIOps}.

Prior literature in cloud reliability typically isolates individual lifecycle phases. Unsupervised anomaly detectors---including Isolation Forests, log-based recurrent architectures \cite{Du2017DeepLog,Meng2019LogAnomaly}, and variational autoencoders \cite{Su2019OmniAnomaly,Xu2018Donut}---attain strong detection accuracy over specific metric streams but remain decoupled from active remediation. Conversely, autonomous self-healing engines \cite{Samir2019SelfHealing,Zhang2022Remediation} trigger reactive mitigation upon static threshold breaches, lacking forward-looking temporal prediction and interpretability. Root-cause analysis (RCA) techniques \cite{Wang2018CloudRanger,Weng2018RCA,Chen2014CauseInfer} reconstruct causal dependencies across distributed traces, yet they require heavy runtime instrumentation and omit closed-loop post-recovery verification.

Consequently, modern operations suffer from fragmentation: disparate specialized modules that are rarely evaluated as a cohesive, end-to-end operational pipeline from live ingestion to post-remediation verification. This investigation resolves this limitation by constructing, validating, and benchmarking a unified platform that connects all operational stages into a reproducible research prototype.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}

\subsection{Cloud Anomaly Detection}

Early cloud monitoring focused on static snapshot telemetry. He et al. \cite{He2016Logs} demonstrated through an empirical benchmarking of classical classifiers on system logs that linear and decision-tree baselines remain competitive against complex architectures on specific subsets. To capture multivariate temporal dynamics in continuous key performance indicators (KPIs), Xu et al. \cite{Xu2018Donut} developed Donut using variational autoencoders for seasonal metrics, attaining F1 scores between 0.75 and 0.90. Su et al. \cite{Su2019OmniAnomaly} introduced OmniAnomaly, leveraging stochastic recurrent units to model temporal metric distributions (0.89 F1 on Server Machine Datasets), while Li et al. \cite{Li2021InterFusion} formulated InterFusion to correlate macroscopic observations with hidden inter-metric dependencies. Although these models detect anomalous deviations effectively, they generate black-box anomaly scalars that neither isolate specific contributory telemetry features nor guide automated remediation.

Sequential log and trace monitoring extends point-in-time checks across discrete execution flows. Du et al. \cite{Du2017DeepLog} developed DeepLog to model normal log sequences through LSTM layers (0.96 F1 on HDFS logs), and Meng et al. \cite{Meng2019LogAnomaly} introduced LogAnomaly to integrate semantic template embeddings with sequential classification (0.98 F1 on BGL logs). In microservice environments, Nedelkoski et al. \cite{Nedelkoski2019Tracing} applied sequence-based learning across distributed trace graphs, achieving 0.95 F1. Despite their diagnostic precision, these methods cannot explain the underlying parametric drivers in continuous telemetry or translate alerts into verifiable remediation workflows.

\subsection{AIOps and Intelligent Operations}

Systematic taxonomies by Lin et al. \cite{Lin2019AIOps} at Microsoft Azure established anomaly detection, root-cause identification, and automated remediation as the core triad of modern AIOps. Bogatinovski et al. \cite{Bogatinovski2022AIOps} and Niu et al. \cite{Niu2020DLAIOps} surveyed deep learning techniques across cloud operations, highlighting the division between isolated diagnostic tools and production execution pipelines. In a broader evaluation of monitoring systems, Islam et al. \cite{Islam2019SLR} observed that while passive anomaly filtering is mature, proactive failure forecasting and closed-loop self-healing frameworks remain under-represented in published experimental literature.

\subsection{Explainability for Operational Systems}

Post-hoc explainability methods provide local interpretations for opaque models. Ribeiro et al. \cite{Ribeiro2016LIME} proposed LIME using local linear surrogates, whereas Lundberg and Lee \cite{Lundberg2017SHAP} formulated SHAP using cooperative game theory to allocate Shapley values. In operational cloud environments, however, both frameworks incur sampling overhead and introduce approximation variance. Li et al. \cite{Li2023XAISurvey} categorized XAI for anomaly detection into post-hoc, model-intrinsic, and feature-based classes. In contrast to sampling approximations, linear models allow exact, closed-form coefficient attribution ($\text{scaled feature} \times \text{weight}$), delivering deterministic explainability with minimal computational cost.

\subsection{Root Cause Analysis}

Structural diagnostic frameworks isolate faulty components through dependency topology. Wang et al. \cite{Wang2018CloudRanger} designed CloudRanger using dynamic service graphs and random-walk traversal, while Chen et al. \cite{Chen2014CauseInfer} introduced CauseInfer to diagnose multi-tier performance degradations (82\% accuracy). Weng et al. \cite{Weng2018RCA} applied constraint-based PC algorithms to discover causal graphs directly from metric streams. While effective, these graph-based methods depend on heavy distributed tracing agents. This work adopts a lighter, non-invasive alternative: aggregating model attribution scores across resource domains (CPU, Memory, Disk, Network) to infer the dominant failure category without requiring tracing infrastructure.

\subsection{Self-Healing and Automated Remediation}

Closed-loop remediation has progressed from static thresholds toward adaptive controls. Samir et al. \cite{Samir2019SelfHealing} demonstrated fuzzy-logic microservice remediation, cutting mean time to repair by 60\% but relying on static heuristic rules without predictive forecasting. Gan et al. \cite{Gan2019Seer} designed Seer, using hardware performance counters to preemptively forecast microservice quality-of-service violations with 91\% accuracy, yet without explainable attribution or root-cause localization. Zhang et al. \cite{Zhang2022Remediation} analyzed automated incident remediation through historical guide recommendations, yet their approach lacks deterministic model-level explanations and does not verify post-recovery efficacy using the predictive classifier.

\subsection{Research Gap and Open Challenges}

The review in Section II reveals that the cloud management literature addresses individual pipeline stages well but rarely evaluates them as a unified end-to-end workflow. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item Detection methods do not explain which features drove the alert.
\item XAI methods are applied to detection models but not connected to recovery actions.
\item Self-healing systems do not use temporal prediction and do not verify recovery with the same model.
\item Survey papers on Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations (AIOps) repeatedly identify system integration as an open challenge, yet they rarely provide integrated experimental evaluations.
\end{enumerate}

This work addresses these gaps by implementing and evaluating an integrated prototype that connects telemetry ingestion, temporal prediction, deterministic XAI attribution, failure-category inference, decision logic, simulated recovery, and model-based verification in a single system. While this system does not claim to be the first to combine cloud monitoring with self-healing, nor does it assert superiority over deep learning anomaly detectors, it provides a comprehensive end-to-end framework demonstrating a complete, interpretable, reproducible workflow in a research prototype setting.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}

This investigation evaluates five core research questions:
\textbf{RQ1} (Detection Fidelity): Can ML anomaly detection identify resource failures across synthetic telemetry?
\textbf{RQ2} (Early Warning): Do temporal rolling features improve lead time over snapshot threshold alarms?
\textbf{RQ3} (Deterministic XAI): Can closed-form coefficient attribution reliably isolate influential telemetry patterns?
\textbf{RQ4} (Remediation Mapping): Can attribution evidence accurately guide automated recovery actions?
\textbf{RQ5} (Closed-Loop Verification): Can model re-evaluation provide an honest, conservative post-healing verification signal?

The primary technical contributions are:
(1)~an end-to-end prototype uniting all ten lifecycle stages in a callable API;
(2)~a 45-feature temporal feature extraction layer strictly eliminating lookahead leakage;
(3)~a rigorous 5-seed comparative evaluation across sequence-isolated splits;
(4)~a closed-form, deterministic XAI attribution engine requiring zero sampling approximations; and
(5)~a closed-loop verification protocol that re-tests remediated states against the original predictive classifier.

\section{Proposed System Architecture and Methodology}

The proposed methodology connects telemetry ingestion, anomaly detection, temporal feature extraction, multi-model failure-risk prediction, deterministic model-coefficient attribution, likely-failure-category inference, autonomous decision logic, and post-recovery verification as a unified sequential pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{figures/fig1_system_architecture.pdf}
\caption{Explainable AI-Driven Cloud Management System Architecture.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The platform comprises ten sequential processing stages:
(1)~\textbf{Telemetry Ingestion} simulates multi-resource metric streams across CPU, memory, storage, and network interfaces;
(2)~\textbf{Anomaly Detection} performs snapshot point-in-time screening via unsupervised Isolation Forest;
(3)~\textbf{Temporal Buffer Management} maintains a 10-step sliding window of historical context;
(4)~\textbf{Feature Extraction} derives 45 leakage-free rolling statistics and linear trends;
(5)~\textbf{Failure Risk Prediction} estimates forward-looking failure probability using regularized Logistic Regression;
(6)~\textbf{XAI Attribution} derives exact signed feature contributions via model weights;
(7)~\textbf{Failure Categorization} aggregates attribution evidence across resource domains;
(8)~\textbf{Decision Logic} maps dominant failure categories to targeted recovery actions;
(9)~\textbf{Simulated Recovery} safely logs remediation directives without destructive cloud calls; and
(10)~\textbf{Post-Recovery Verification} re-evaluates simulated restored telemetry using the identical predictive classifier.

\subsection{Temporal Feature Engineering}

A leak-free temporal feature set comprising 45 features is constructed from each 10-step trailing window. For each of the five raw metrics, nine features are computed: current value, 5-step mean, 10-step mean, 5-step delta, 10-step delta, 5-step slope (via linear regression), 10-step slope, 5-step standard deviation, and 10-step standard deviation. The feature extraction module strictly utilizes trailing observations, ensuring no access to future values (thereby preventing data leakage). StandardScaler is fit only on training rows and applied consistently during live inference.

\subsection{Predictive Modeling and Target Formulation}

Logistic Regression was selected for the integrated system with configuration: C=1.0, max\_iter=2000, random\_state=42, class\_weight='balanced'. The prediction target is strictly forward-looking and binary: a label of 1 denotes that an active failure occurs within the subsequent 10 future timesteps ($t+1$ to $t+10$), strictly excluding the current observation at timestep $t$ to avoid target circularity, and 0 otherwise.

The model is trained at server initialisation on the Phase 3C seed-42 training split (4,920 rows from 120 sequences). The fitted scaler and model are used for all live inference. Logistic Regression is selected over Random Forest and Gradient Boosting not because it achieves the highest aggregate performance --- all three achieve similar F1 and AUC within measurement noise --- but because its coefficient-based attribution is mathematically exact and requires no post-hoc approximation method.

\subsection{Explainable AI and Failure Categorization}

\subsubsection{XAI Attribution}
For each live prediction, the contribution of feature $i$ is computed according to (\ref{eq:contrib}):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contrib}
\text{contribution}_i = \text{scaled\_value}_i \times \text{coefficient}_i
\end{equation}

where $\text{contribution}_i$ denotes the signed contribution of feature $i$ to the predicted log-odds of failure, $\text{scaled\_value}_i = (x_i - \mu_i)/\sigma_i$ is the $z$-score normalized feature value computed via the training-set StandardScaler, and $\text{coefficient}_i$ is the corresponding learned Logistic Regression weight. A positive contribution increases the predicted failure probability toward the high-risk threshold, whereas a negative contribution decreases it. The top 5 features by absolute contribution are reported for each prediction. This method is deterministic and computationally inexpensive.

\subsubsection{Likely Failure Category Inference}
Evidence scores for four resource categories (CPU, Memory, Disk, Network) are computed by summing absolute contributions of features belonging to each resource. The category with the highest summed evidence is reported as the likely failure category. This is a heuristic inference from model evidence, not a causal determination.

\subsection{Post-Healing Verification Protocol}

When predicted failure risk triggers a HIGH or CRITICAL alert, the system executes an automated verification cycle: (1)~pre-remediation telemetry and risk states are recorded; (2)~the decision engine dispatches the mapped recovery action (simulated string selection without destructive infrastructure calls); (3)~a 10-step stable telemetry sequence is synthesized as a recovery proxy; (4)~the identical trained Logistic Regression classifier re-evaluates the post-recovery window; and (5)~a tripartite verification verdict is determined: \textbf{RECOVERED} if post-healing risk drops to LOW ($\le 0.25$), \textbf{NOT\_RECOVERED} if risk remains elevated ($\ge 0.50$), or \textbf{INCONCLUSIVE} if risk hovers in the intermediate range ($0.25 < \text{risk} < 0.50$). The sliding-window buffer is subsequently flushed, returning the runtime loop to the WARMING\_UP state.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset Configuration and Splits}

The evaluation dataset encompasses eight distinct scenario types: two normal baseline profiles (\texttt{normal\_stable} and \texttt{normal\_variable}) and six failure scenarios (\texttt{cpu\_sudden\_failure}, \texttt{cpu\_gradual\_failure}, \texttt{memory\_gradual\_leak}, \texttt{disk\_gradual\_exhaustion}, \texttt{network\_degradation}, and \texttt{combined\_gradual\_failure}). Across these eight scenarios, a total of 180 sequences were generated and partitioned into 120 training sequences (4,920 supervised rows), 30 validation sequences (1,230 supervised rows), and 30 test sequences (1,230 supervised rows), yielding exactly 7,380 supervised rows under a 10-step trailing window and 10-step prediction horizon censoring ($60 - 10 - 10 + 1 = 41$ rows per 60-step sequence), as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:splits}.

Sequence-level splitting ensures no temporal window from a test sequence is visible during training. Zero sequence overlap is verified by an automated audit (evaluation\_audit.json).

\textbf{Positive label definition:} A timestep $t$ is labelled positive ($y_t = 1$) if an active failure is present in any of the next 10 future observations ($t+1 \le t' \le t+10$). The current observation at timestep $t$ is strictly excluded from the target calculation, ensuring that the model is trained exclusively on advance predictive indicators rather than instantaneous state detection. Positive samples constitute approximately 52\% of the dataset under this early-warning formulation.

\subsection{Anomaly Detection Evaluation (Phase 2)}

Isolation Forest is evaluated on snapshot telemetry. Baselines include a fixed-threshold classifier (alarm if any metric exceeds 80\%) evaluated on identical data.

\subsection{Temporal Predictive Evaluation (Phase 3)}

Five model architectures were benchmarked across five independent random seeds \{42, 123, 456, 789, 2026\}: (1)~majority-class baseline; (2)~current-value threshold baseline (flagging when any metric exceeds 80\%); (3)~balanced Logistic Regression ($C=1.0$, $\text{max\_iter}=2000$); (4)~balanced Random Forest (100 estimators); and (5)~Gradient Boosting (100 estimators). Performance was quantified via accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC.

\subsection{Early-Warning Horizon Evaluation}

Among test sequences containing labelled failure regions, the number of timesteps by which the model issues its first HIGH or CRITICAL prediction prior to the labelled failure onset is measured.

\subsection{Explainability Evaluation Protocol}

Four representative XAI case studies are documented from test sequences spanning different scenarios. For each case, the top 5 contributing features, their directions, and the evidence scores are recorded.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Temporal Early Warning and Sensitivity Trade-offs}

The experimental findings confirm that rolling temporal features provide substantially superior early-warning sensitivity compared with snapshot threshold monitoring. The threshold baseline achieves recall of only 8.6\% (mean across seeds) because gradual degradations accumulate beneath static alarm boundaries until late in the failure progression. Conversely, Logistic Regression trained on multi-window features attains 66.1\% mean recall with an average lead time of 18.06 timesteps. In production deployments, however, this high sensitivity involves a documented operational trade-off: false positive alerts during benign bursty periods (\texttt{normal\_stable}), which require threshold dampening or alert coalescing.

\subsection{Interpretability Guarantees in Autonomous Operations}

While ensemble architectures (Random Forest and Gradient Boosting) attained marginally higher mean F1 scores (0.696 and 0.699 vs 0.692), Logistic Regression was deliberately selected as the authoritative operational model. In mission-critical cloud remediation, execution transparency is paramount: Logistic Regression provides exact, closed-form linear attribution ($w_i \cdot \tilde{x}_i$) without requiring sampling-based approximations (such as Kernel-SHAP or surrogate LIME models). When operators receive an automated recommendation, individual feature contributions and resource evidence scores can be audited deterministically with zero runtime approximation latency.

\subsection{Attribution Boundaries and Verification Honesty}

Aggregating attribution scores across resource domains reliably isolates primary fault categories for gradual failure modes (CPU, memory, disk). However, this heuristic approach cannot establish counterfactual causal relationships and exhibits lower diagnostic resolution on compound, multi-resource failures. Furthermore, the post-recovery verification mechanism conservatively returned an \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE} verdict during network degradation evaluation (risk reduced from 0.884 to 0.447). Rather than overstating recovery success, the tripartite verdict structure (\texttt{RECOVERED}, \texttt{NOT\_RECOVERED}, \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE}) enforces essential academic and operational honesty.

\section{System Limitations and Threats to Validity}

Key constraints must be explicitly acknowledged:
(1)~\textbf{Synthetic Telemetry:} Telemetry is generated via parameterised stochastic distributions; validation on physical multi-tenant cloud hardware remains necessary.
(2)~\textbf{Simulated Remediation:} Recovery operations are logged mock directives rather than destructive hypervisor or cloud provider API calls.
(3)~\textbf{Sudden Failure Bounds:} Abrupt step-function spikes exhibit lower predictive sensitivity (F1 0.529) due to the absence of precursor trend signals.
(4)~\textbf{Non-Causal Attribution:} Resource-level evidence aggregation reflects model focus rather than formal counterfactual causal mechanisms.
(5)~\textbf{Synthetic Recovery Proxy:} Post-healing verification evaluates restored synthetic normal streams rather than physical platform convalescence.
(6)~\textbf{Runtime Initialisation:} The research prototype fits the model at server startup, which is appropriate for evaluation but requires dedicated model registries in production.
(7)~\textbf{Empirical Scope:} Benchmarking against production traces (e.g., Google or Alibaba cluster datasets) represents crucial future validation.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This paper demonstrated an end-to-end, explainable autonomous cloud management platform integrating ten lifecycle stages from telemetry ingestion through post-remediation verification. Experimental evaluation confirms: (1)~Isolation Forest achieves F1 0.760 on snapshot telemetry; (2)~temporal Logistic Regression attains mean F1 0.692 and ROC-AUC 0.813, substantially exceeding snapshot threshold baselines (F1 0.156); (3)~temporal rolling features deliver an advance early-warning lead time averaging 18.06 timesteps prior to failure onset; (4)~deterministic coefficient attribution isolates influential slope and variability dynamics without sampling approximations; and (5)~the closed-loop verification engine enforces an honest, conservative standard by reporting INCONCLUSIVE on partial remediations.

Future work will transition this framework to physical cloud testbeds, integrate real orchestration APIs (e.g., Kubernetes operators), examine multi-model attribution consensus, and benchmark deep sequence architectures against this interpretable linear baseline.
